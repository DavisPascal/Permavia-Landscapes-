Much of East Africa receives generous rain, yet many homes and farms still run short of water in the dry months. The problem is rarely how much rain falls. It is how quickly that water leaves the land. Regenerative design slows it down, spreads it out and sinks it into the soil, where plants can use it for months.

## How much rain can your roof collect?

A typical 100 square metre roof collects about 1,000 litres for every 10 mm of rain. Gutters, a first-flush diverter and a covered tank turn that into clean water for the house, the kitchen garden and young trees. Overflow is then directed into the landscape rather than onto the road.

## What is a swale, and how does it store water?

A swale is a shallow ditch dug exactly along the contour, with the soil piled on the downhill side to form a mound. Rain running down the slope is caught, spreads along the swale and soaks in. Trees planted on the mound tap into that moisture long after the rain stops.

- Lay out swales with a simple A-frame or water level, never by eye.
- Plant the mound immediately with fruit trees, nitrogen fixers and groundcover.
- Always build a safe, stable spillway for heavy storms.

## Ponds and infiltration basins

Where the land allows, a pond stores surface water for animals, irrigation and fish, and it cools the area around it. Smaller infiltration basins near the house catch runoff from paths and yards and recharge the groundwater.

> The cheapest place to store water is in healthy, living soil. Every 1% increase in organic matter helps a hectare hold tens of thousands of extra litres.

## Where should you begin?

Observe your land in the next heavy rain. Where does water run, pool or cut channels? Those are the places to act first. If you would like a water plan for your site, our team can walk it with you and design a system matched to your slope, soil and budget.
