Transforming Spaces: The Art of Green Design

Permavia Team 4 Aug 2026 5 min read
In short

Good green design starts by observing a site (sun, wind, water and how people move) before changing anything. Permaculture then organises the land into zones by how often you visit them, and agroforestry uses trees as the backbone, providing shade, fruit, fodder, timber and mulch while feeding and holding the soil.

Every site has a story written in its slopes, soils and shade. Green design begins by reading that story before changing anything. The result is a space that fits its place, rather than one that fights it.

Observe before you design

We spend time understanding how sun moves across a site through the day and the year, where wind comes from, where water flows and pools, and how people actually move through the space. Those observations shape every decision that follows.

What are permaculture zones?

Permaculture design organises a site into zones by how often you visit them. Herbs and vegetables you use daily sit closest to the kitchen. Fruit trees come next, then larger woodlots and wilder areas further out. This simple idea saves time, water and energy every single day.

Why are trees the backbone of agroforestry?

In agroforestry, trees are the backbone of the design. They provide shade, fruit, fodder, timber and mulch, fix nitrogen and hold the soil together. Crops and animals are woven around them so that each element supports several others.

  • Choose indigenous and well-adapted species first.
  • Mix nitrogen-fixing trees among fruit and timber trees.
  • Plan for the mature size of each tree, not its size at planting.

Designing for change

A green space is never finished. It grows, matures and changes over decades. Good design anticipates that journey, so the landscape becomes more productive and more beautiful with time, and needs less from you as it does.

Frequently asked questions

What are permaculture zones?

A way of placing things by how often you use them: herbs and vegetables you pick daily sit closest to the kitchen, fruit trees come next, then woodlots and wilder areas further out. It saves time, water and energy every day.

Why are trees important in agroforestry?

Trees provide shade, fruit, fodder, timber and mulch, fix nitrogen and hold the soil together, so crops and animals woven around them do better.

Which trees should I plant first?

Indigenous and well-adapted species first, with nitrogen-fixing trees mixed among fruit and timber trees. Always plan for each tree’s mature size, not its size at planting.

What should I do before designing my garden or farm?

Observe it: how the sun moves through the day and year, where the wind comes from, where water flows and pools, and how people move through the space.
